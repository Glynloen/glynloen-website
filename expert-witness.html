Qualifications

Recognized in state and federal courts.

Rick Bingham has been recognized as a qualified expert in state and federal courts. He served 14 years on the Hearing Board of the Illinois Attorney Registration and Disciplinary Commission, adjudicating serious attorney-misconduct cases, and has also served on its Oversight Board.

Daubert & Rule 702 challenges

No court has ever excluded Rick Bingham as an expert, under Daubert, Rule 702, or any state standards.

For identified assignments, work-product samples, including deposition & trial transcripts, state & federal court reports, and expert disclosures, are available to prospective clients on request.

We relish the challenge of explaining the issues and their applicability to the conduct of the parties, and their consistency with, or deviation from, the standard of conduct or practice for complex insurance problems.

On every engagement, GIC examines the record using case-management software, develops initial proto-opinions, flags the facts that support and contradict each position, and produces thoroughly cited reports, then provides deposition and trial testimony.

That review runs on GIC’s tech-assisted case-mapping process: every material fact placed on a timeline, every finding traced back to a Bates number.

Representative engagements

A track record built on high-stakes, complex matters.

  • Department of Financial Services: retained to review the claims practices of an insurer in receivership, in a damages case against the insurer’s Directors and Officers and their outside auditor. The analysis covered corporate governance, claim reserving, communication with claimants, and adherence to fair-claims-practice rules & file-documentation standards.
  • Third-party claims administrator: defense of a federal action alleging breach of contract and incompetent claim handling. Relying on GIC’s analysis, the client disposed of the case by dispositive motion.
  • Cedent in a facultative reinsurer dispute: review of a 40,000+ page tier-3 asbestos record addressing late notice, proper reserving, and the reinsurer’s rights of inspection & participation. A 70-page report authored in under five weeks, an 80-page rebuttal report, and deposition testimony.
  • Primary carrier sued by an excess carrier for equitable subrogation for bad faith and allegations of statutory bad faith: a 94-page report with 445 citations and a 57-page rebuttal report with 142 citations disputing the allegations.
  • Law firm in a defense-negligence case: the excess carrier alleged that the defense of a maritime claim was flawed, that the case was inadequately prepared for trial, and that counsel gave late notice to the excess carrier.
